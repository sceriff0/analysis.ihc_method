Starting data and notation

ANHIR (Automatic Non-rigid Histological Image Registration, anhir.grand-challenge.org) is the public benchmark for registering serially-sectioned, differently-stained histology images. It ships pairs of whole-slide images at several downsampled scales with pathologist-placed landmarks, so a method’s error is measured against human correspondences rather than against its own features — the one thing neither the synthetic sweep nor the run QC can offer. Here mirage’s two backends, VALIS and STARE (tiled), are scored on it beside the challenge’s own bUnwarpJ baseline and the initial (unregistered) state.

Where the numbers come from. mirage’s benchmarks/anhir/ harness registers every ANHIR pair with each method and scores the result; its benchmarks/pull_to_ihc_method.sh copies two tables into data/benchmark/:

  • anhir_cases.csv — one row per (case, method): the case’s tissue, scale and status, then n_landmarks, rtre_median / rtre_mean / rtre_max, tre_median_px, robustness, rank_median_rtre and time_min.
  • anhir_aggregates.csv — one row per (method, subset), for the subsets all, training, evaluation, robust (cases with robustness > 0.5) and one tissue:<name> per tissue: the means and medians of the per-case metrics plus avg_rank_median_rtre.

The metrics, as the challenge defines them (Performance Metrics):

  • TRE — target registration error: for each landmark, the distance in pixels between the warped source landmark and its target.
  • rTRE — relative TRE: TRE divided by the target image’s diagonal. Unitless, so it compares across image sizes and scales; a good non-rigid result sits around 10-3, no registration around 10-1. Every figure below uses rTRE.
  • Robustness — the fraction of a case’s landmarks whose rTRE improved over the initial (unregistered) state. A case is “robust” above 0.5.
  • Ranking — the challenge’s primary metric. On each case the methods are ranked by their median rTRE (1 = best), and each method’s rank is averaged over cases; lower is better. avg_rank_median_rtre for subset == "all" is that number.

Only training cases are scored here. ANHIR releases target landmarks for the training split only; evaluation cases are scored server-side on grand-challenge.org from submitted warped landmarks. So in anhir_cases.csv evaluation rows carry scored = FALSE and NA metrics, every figure draws the training cases, and the evaluation aggregate row is empty until a submission is scored on the server.

Notes

  • Landmarks, not self-assessment. The sweep page and the run-QC page score a registration with the features the method itself matched (VALIS rTRE, STARE tile error) or with an independent DAPI overlap; ANHIR scores it against pathologist-placed correspondences, so it is the one benchmark here where the error is external to every method.
  • Training split only. The evaluation split’s landmarks are withheld by the challenge; its rows are present in anhir_cases.csv so the case inventory is complete, but unscored. A server-side score would arrive as a leaderboard entry, not through this page.
  • Rank is the headline, rTRE is the reason. The challenge ranks on mean rank of per-case median rTRE (§4); the distributions (§1–§2) and robustness (§3) say why a method ranks where it does — a method can win the rank while losing a tissue.
  • This is not the study’s tissue. ANHIR’s pairs are differently-stained serial sections at reduced scale; the study’s cyclic-IF rounds are the same section re-imaged. For the registration accuracy of the run that produced this study’s cells, see run QC and registration arms.
