This site collects the reproducible analyses behind the IHC method study — spatial single-cell IHC, bulk RNA-seq, cell-type deconvolution, clinical data, and the image-registration / segmentation benchmarks that validate the pipeline.

The single-cell inputs come from the sibling repos: cells are segmented and quantified by mirage (../mirage) and gated independently in QuPath by FlowPath (../qupath-extension-flowpath); the benchmark tables come from mirage’s benchmarks/ sweep. code/cell_tables.R reconciles the export schemas and the two phenotype vocabularies those tools produce, so every analysis here reads one vocabulary — see code/README.md.

How the site is organised

Four parts, in the order the study is read. Each part is one claim, and the navbar has one entry per part.

part claim pages
1 · Clinical cells land in the right region, and FlowPath’s phenotypes agree with the pathologist clinical_flowpath, clinical_membership_qc
2 · Molecular bulk RNA, deconvolution and the invasive margin agree with the slide, and with the clinical hot/cold call molecular_hot_cold
3 · Registration the study slides were registered well enough to analyse, under a configuration chosen on real tissue registration_run_qc, registration_arms
4 · Benchmarks the pipeline’s cost and accuracy scale as claimed, measured on synthetic images benchmark_registration, benchmark_pipeline

The filename prefix is the part: anything clinical_* belongs to part 1, registration_* to part 3, benchmark_* to part 4. Two pages sit outside the four — paper_figures (the manuscript assembly sheet) and marker_qc (internal gating QC that everything else assumes has passed).

Every page defines SLUG equal to its own filename and calls export_pdf_figures(SLUG) last, so a page’s vector figures always land in output/figures/<page name>/.

Building the manuscript figures

  • Paper figures — the panel assembly sheet. One panel per plot at publication proportions, exported as vector PDFs to output/figures/paper_figures/ for assembly in Affinity, plus a named slot with acquisition instructions for every panel that has to be a screenshot or a microscope crop. Reads the all-slide export (data/all_slide/); knit it to see which panels are ready and which are still missing their input.

Part 1 — Clinical data and neoplastic concordance

Phenotypes come from FlowPath’s QuPath gate tree; membership from the pathologist’s own polygons, region by region.

  • Clinical concordance — FlowPath — the main clinical page. A cell is inside when its centroid falls in that region’s own polygon (sf point-in-polygon), because the all-slide export pairs a cell csv with a geojson region by region. A patient with no annotation directory is entirely inside — the layout’s own convention, marked whole_slide rather than guessed. Includes the concordance against the pathologist’s neoplastic-cellularity score.
  • Annotation membership QC (in progress) — sf point-in-polygon membership vs. the Out_of_annotation flag, compared per cell. This is the check the page above depends on: if membership itself is wrong, nothing downstream of it means anything.

Part 2 — Bulk RNA, deconvolution and the hot/cold axis

  • Bulk RNA, deconvolution and the hot/cold axis — one page, three parts, one question: does a bulk readout agree with the slide, and does that agreement depend on whether the tumour is immunologically hot or cold? Part 1 is per-marker bulk expression vs the IHC positive-cell fraction; part 2 is immunedeconv cell-type scores vs the IHC lineage fractions; part 3 is the invasive-margin band (CD45+ fraction and density) against the clinical HOT score and immuno-phenotype. Points in parts 1 and 2 are coloured by that same clinical call, so a readout that only tracks IHC in hot tumours is visible as a colour split rather than hidden inside a middling correlation.

Part 3 — Registration accuracy on the study run

These two read the real slides. They answer different questions and are not interchangeable; registration arms is the one the manuscript needs.

  • Run QC — the study samples — the registration QC of the run that produced the analysed data: VALIS’s or STARE’s own registration error (including STARE’s per-tile spatial map) and the independent DAPI-nucleus overlap cross-check. Reads data/mirage/ and needs nothing else — the only page that renders from a pipeline run alone, with no counts, no clinical table and no gating.
  • Registration arms — the study slides registered once per configuration (memory_mode high/low crossed with reg_micro_reg 0/1/2 = six arms). Ranks the arms on real tissue and separates what each knob buys. Also where the comparability rules live: mirage’s rigid QC stage means affine ∘ micro-rigid at depth ≥ 1, so it cannot be compared across depths.

Part 4 — Benchmarks

Both read mirage’s benchmarks/ sweep, which runs on synthetic images with a known injected transform to measure cost and scaling. They say nothing about your samples — part 3 does that.

  • Registration accuracy — synthetic sweep — VALIS feature rTRE per stage, the segmentation-overlap cross-check, accuracy vs cost, and an agreement view of the two estimates.
  • Pipeline benchmarks — resource scaling, I/O volume, whole-pipeline cost and segmentation-method comparison.

Marker QC (internal, no external reference)

  • Marker QC — whether the FlowPath gating is self-consistent before anything is compared against it: per-channel usability and bimodality, phenotype ↔︎ marker agreement, morphology by phenotype, then lineage leakage (CD45 firing inside tumour phenotypes, PANCK inside immune ones) and marker-pair exclusivity. Replaces the former ihc_qc and exclusivity pages, which asked the same question of the same table from two directions.
